# DEAD PEDAL

A cooperative zombie-survival cycling game prototype. Your pedalling is the
controller: keep your speed up, stay together, and escape the town before the
horde catches the group.

**Status:** playable prototype. Three modes: the story mission *Escape the
Town* and **Endless Survival** (both single-player or 2–6 player
LAN/host-and-join multiplayer), and **Criterium**, a zombie-free points race
against computer riders (single-player). Ride with a
**Bluetooth smart trainer** (e.g. Zwift Hub One) through the included trainer
bridge, or with the **keyboard simulation** for development.

---

## 1. Running the game

1. Install **Godot 4.7** (standard build, not .NET) from
   <https://godotengine.org/download>. Godot 4.4+ should also work.
2. Start Godot, click **Import**, and select `project.godot` in this folder.
3. Press **F5** (or the ▶ Play button, top-right) to run the game.

The first time you open the project, Godot imports it, which takes a few
seconds. Everything (town, models, sounds) is generated by code, so there are
no asset files to download.

### Building a stand-alone Windows .exe

The project includes a "Windows Desktop" export preset that produces a single
self-contained `DeadPedal.exe` (Godot isn't needed to run it):

- In the editor: **Project → Export… → Windows Desktop → Export Project**.
  The first time, Godot asks you to download the export templates
  (**Editor → Manage Export Templates → Download and Install**).
- Or from a terminal: `godot --headless --path . --export-release "Windows Desktop" build/windows/DeadPedal.exe`

`build/` is ignored by git, so builds aren't committed.

### Controls (development: simulated cycling)

| Key | Simulates |
|---|---|
| `1` – `5` | Steady effort: recovery, easy, normal, hard, all-out (fractions of your FTP) |
| `0` or `` ` `` | Stop pedalling |
| `W` / `↑` (hold) | Push harder; release and power falls back to your steady level |
| `Space` (hold) | Sprint, with a fast ramp to max power |
| `S` / `↓` (hold) | Stop pedalling immediately |
| `F1` | Show/hide the input help panel |

A typical ride: press **3** (normal) to cruise, hold **Space** when the horde
surges, and press **4** or hold **W** at the gate.

### The mission: *Escape the Town* (~3–4 minutes)

1. **Departure:** ride out through the abandoned town.
2. **Pursuit:** the horde spots you. The HORDE meter (bottom-right) shows how
   close it is. It occasionally **surges**, so sprint!
3. **Locked gate:** you stop at the quarantine gate. Keep pedalling: everyone's
   effort winds the winch. The green lamps on the gate and the bar show progress
   while the horde shuffles closer.
4. **Final escape:** the gate opens; race to the green flare at the extraction point.
5. **Results:** time, distance, top speed, gate objective, and **Replay**.

The horde hangs back at a distance that depends on how fast the **rearmost**
rider goes: ride harder and it drops back (up to ~45 m), ease off and it creeps
closer. It can't close in faster than 3 m/s, so you always get a few seconds of
warning. If it reaches a rider, they're **grabbed**, and they have 3 seconds to
sprint free before the group is lost. The group is only as safe as its slowest
member.

### Endless Survival

A road that never ends, through changing areas: the old town, the outskirts,
forest and open farmland. Ride as far as you can.

- **The horde gets faster** the further you go, and surges more often. Every
  run ends eventually; stronger, smarter riding lasts longer.
- **Checkpoint gates** every 2 km: pedal together to wind them open. Each one
  needs a bit more effort than the last.
- **Survivors** wait by the road, waving a red flare (green marks on the
  progress bar). **Slow to 20 km/h** as you pass to pick one up (up to 3).
  - They take turns **on the front**: ride 2–10 m behind one for a +10%
    slipstream. Sprint past them and they tuck in behind you for a while.
  - Pulling tires them out (their energy shows on the left of the screen).
    An **exhausted** survivor drops back towards the horde: ease off and let
    them catch your wheel (ride just in front of them) for a few seconds and
    they recover. Leave them and the horde gets them.
  - They help wind the gates, and when the horde grabs you, a survivor is
    taken instead (an exhausted one first). Sprint during the grab to save
    them too.
- **Screamers** (purple marks) stand by the road with their arms up. Pass them
  **faster than 30 km/h** or they shriek and the horde surges.
- **Supply crates** (red or yellow smoke): slow to 20 km/h to grab them.
  **Flares** drive the horde back; **food** gives your survivors their energy
  back.
- **Runners** break out of the horde now and then after the first checkpoint
  ("RUNNER!" and a red warning in the horde panel). Sprint before one reaches
  you, or it knocks you off your rhythm (you lose a third of your speed).
  Runners also go for exhausted survivors.
- **Score** = metres ridden + 500 per checkpoint + 250 per survivor rescued.
  The best 10 runs are saved on your PC; your best shows on the title screen.
- Every run has a new random world. In multiplayer, everyone gets the same one.

### Criterium (points race, no zombies)

A race on a ~560 m town circuit on a sunny race day, against 3, 5 or 7
computer riders. **The first rider across the line on each lap scores a point;
every 5th lap is a bell lap worth 2. First to 5, 10 or 15 points wins.** A race
to 5 points takes about 10–15 minutes, to 10 points 20–30.

- **Real-world physics** (criterium only - the zombie modes keep their
  snappier arcade model): your watts push against your weight, rolling
  resistance and air drag, so speed builds up gradually and you coast when you
  stop pedalling. Riding alone on the flat: ~26 km/h at 100 W, ~33 km/h at
  200 W, ~38 km/h at 300 W. The computer riders ride at percentages of *your*
  FTP, so "Equal" means equal. Sprints count up to 320% of FTP.
- **Slipstream:** right behind someone (within ~1.5 m) you have 35% less air
  drag, fading to nothing by 8 m (a bit more deeper in a line). The HUD shows
  *SHELTERED -35% wind behind Dan*, *IN THE WIND* or *DROPPED*.
- **Draft help** (title screen: off / normal / strong, default normal), like
  Zwift's "sticky" draft: when you're right behind someone and your power is
  roughly what it takes to follow (normal: within about 30%), you're held on
  their wheel, and a little too much power touches the brakes instead of
  riding past. Surge clearly to come past, ease off clearly to drop back. The
  HUD shows *ON DAN'S WHEEL* while it's holding you.
- **Sprint battery:** everyone (you and the computer riders) has one. Riding
  above your FTP drains it; easy riding refills it. When it's empty you can't
  go above FTP until it recovers, so you can't win every lap. Pick your laps.
- **Power display:** watts are shown as a 3-second average (all modes), like
  other cycling apps - trainers report power in noisy bursts.
- **Computer riders** follow the same rules, with personalities: *diesels*
  pull on the front, *sprinters* sit in and launch ~200 m out, *attackers* go
  early, *wheelsuckers* never lead, *opportunists* wait for bell laps. They
  react to attacks (a quick one can open a gap), chase breaks, take turns on
  the front, regroup after the sprint, and fight for every lap near the end.
- **HUD:** lap and metres to the line, the points table, a live map of the
  circuit with every rider, your shelter, and your battery.
- Choose points, number of rivals and their strength (*Easier / Equal /
  Harder*) under the CRITERIUM button on the title screen.

## 1b. Riding with a smart trainer (Zwift Hub One and others)

Godot can't talk to Bluetooth by itself, so a small helper program, the
**trainer bridge**, connects to your trainer and passes your power and cadence
to the game. It works with any trainer that broadcasts the standard Bluetooth
**FTMS** or **Cycling Power** profile: Zwift Hub One/Hub, Wahoo KICKR, Tacx,
Elite, JetBlack and others. It only *reads* data and never changes resistance.

The bridge is a single Windows program, `DeadPedalTrainerBridge.exe`, included
in the Windows download next to the game. There's nothing to install. Your PC
needs Bluetooth (built in, or a USB Bluetooth adapter).

**Every ride:**
1. **Close Zwift** and any other app connected to the trainer. Most trainers
   accept only one Bluetooth connection for this data.
2. Pedal a few strokes to wake the trainer up.
3. Double-click `DeadPedalTrainerBridge.exe`. When it connects, the window
   shows your live watts. **Leave it open while you ride.** (Windows may warn
   about an unknown publisher the first time: **More info → Run anyway**.)
4. Start the game. On the title screen, set **Cycling input** to **Smart
   trainer**. The status line below it shows your live power; check it moves
   when you pedal. This setting is remembered.
5. Set your real **FTP** on the title screen so effort is scaled to you.

**Virtual gears with a Zwift Click** (optional)

If a Zwift Click is nearby, the bridge connects to it as well. Its buttons then
change virtual gears, like Zwift's virtual shifting: your power still sets
your in-game speed, but a higher gear makes the trainer heavier at the same
cadence. The bridge does this by setting the trainer's simulated slope (FTMS),
a little per gear (24 gears; gear 8 feels like a flat road).

- **Zwift Click (original):** `+` = harder gear, `-` = easier gear.
- **Zwift Click v2:** use the **right** puck: `+` or `Y` = harder, `B` or
  `Z` = easier. Zwift locks the left puck to its own app, so it can't be used.
- Press a button on the Click to wake it, and make sure it isn't connected to
  Zwift or the Companion app.
- The current gear shows on screen (turn this off with **Show gear on screen**
  on the title screen). The bridge window shows it too.
- Without a Click, the bridge never changes the trainer's resistance. When you
  close the bridge window, it puts the trainer back on a flat road.
- Feel of the gears: from a command prompt, `DeadPedalTrainerBridge.exe
  -gear-step 0.5` (slope per gear, default 0.4 %), `-neutral-gear 10`,
  `-gear-count 24`, or `-gears=false` to ignore the Click.
- If the Click doesn't respond, double-click `Bridge with debug log.bat`,
  press its buttons, and send the window's text to the developer.

**Troubleshooting**
- *"No trainer found"*: make sure Zwift/the companion app is closed, pedal to
  wake the trainer, and keep the PC within a few metres of it. Double-click
  `Find trainers.bat` to see what the bridge can find.
- *Several trainers nearby*: run `DeadPedalTrainerBridge.exe -name "Zwift"`
  from a command prompt to pick by name.
- *"TRAINER SIGNAL LOST"* in game: the bridge window closed or lost the
  trainer. It reconnects automatically.
- *Testing without a trainer*: `Test without trainer.bat` sends fake data,
  which the game clearly labels as **SIMULATED**.

**Building the bridge** (only needed if you change it): it's written in Go
(`tools/trainer_bridge`, using the `tinygo.org/x/bluetooth` library). With Go
installed: `cd tools/trainer_bridge` then
`GOOS=windows GOARCH=amd64 go build -o DeadPedalTrainerBridge.exe .`
(in Windows PowerShell: `$env:GOOS="windows"; go build -o DeadPedalTrainerBridge.exe .`).
- Multiplayer: each rider runs the bridge on their own PC. Two copies of the
  game on **one** PC can't both use a trainer.

## 2. Multiplayer (2–6 players)

**On one computer (for testing):** run two copies of the game.

- In the Godot editor, open **Debug → Customize Run Instances…**, tick
  **Enable Multiple Instances**, set the count to **2**, and press F5. Two
  windows open.
- Window A: **HOST GAME**. Window B: **JOIN GAME** (IP `127.0.0.1`).
- In window A, press **START MISSION**. Each window responds only to the
  keyboard while it has focus, so click a window before "pedalling" in it.

**On two computers on the same network (LAN):**

- Host: click **HOST GAME**. The lobby shows this computer's LAN IP address(es).
- Other player: type that IP into the box next to **JOIN GAME** and click it.
- The host may need to allow Godot through the firewall (UDP port 7777).

**Over the internet:** not tested yet. It needs the host to forward UDP port
7777 on their router (or a VPN such as Tailscale/ZeroTier, which makes it
behave like a LAN). This prototype has no matchmaking or relay server.

**How it's synchronised:**
- The host is authoritative for the mission: stage, horde position, gate
  progress, success/failure.
- Each player is authoritative only for their own cyclist (driven by their own
  input) and sends its distance/speed/effort 20×/s.
- Solo play runs exactly the same code path, using Godot's offline peer.

## 3. Fairness: why a weaker rider isn't a liability

- **Effort is relative to your FTP.** Set your FTP on the title screen. Speed
  comes from *effort as a fraction of your own fitness*, so a 150 W-FTP rider
  at threshold is as fast as a 300 W-FTP rider at threshold.
- **Slipstream:** riding 1.5–10 m behind a teammate gives +10% speed, so
  stronger riders can pull others along.
- **The gate adds up the team's effort**, so a strong rider can carry more of
  the load. Teams need more total effort than a solo rider, but less than
  double, so working together pays off.

### Tuning (all in the Inspector)

- **Mission timings and distances:** select the `Mission` root node in
  `escape_the_town.tscn` (gate distance, extraction distance, pursuit start, …).
- **Horde difficulty:** the `Zombies` node (speeds, surges, catch-up, danger range).
- **Gate difficulty:** the `Gate` node (effort-seconds per rider, team scaling).
- **Speed feel:** open `cyclist.tscn`, select the root, expand **Ride Model**.
- **Camera:** the `Camera` node.
- **Road shape:** the `Route` node's **Control Points**.
- **Endless mode:** select the `Mission` root in `endless_survival.tscn`:
  checkpoint spacing, how fast the horde speeds up (`Pace Increase Per Km`,
  `Max Pace`), gate effort, how often survivors / screamers / crates appear
  (`Roadside`), survivor energy (`Front Drain`, `Group Recovery`,
  `Wheel Recovery`...), runners (`Runners From`, `Runner Interval`,
  `Runner Slowdown`) and score values. Runner speed is on the `Zombies` node
  (`Runner Speed Bonus`, `Runner Max Speed`).
- **Criterium:** select the `Race` root in `race/criterium.tscn` for the
  bell laps, slipstream (`Max Draft Bonus` = air drag saved, `Draft
  Range`...), battery (`Battery Capacity`, `Battery Recharge`, `Max Effort`)
  and the physics (`Physics`: rider + bike mass, drag area, rolling
  resistance). The circuit's shape is the `Route`
  node's `Corners` and `Corner Radii`. Rider personalities are the `PROFILES`
  table at the top of `scripts/race/race_bot.gd`.

### How the cycling input works

Every input is a `CyclingInputSource` (`scripts/input/`): keyboard
simulation, scripted test input, and `TrainerBridgeInput`, which receives
`{"power", "cadence", "device", "simulated"}` JSON packets on UDP
127.0.0.1:47800 from the trainer bridge (`tools/trainer_bridge`). To support
another kind of device (e.g. ANT+ FE-C), either extend the bridge or add a new
`CyclingInputSource` subclass. Movement, zombies, missions and networking
don't need to change.

## 5. Testing

Unit tests (input, ride model, route, gate, horde):

```
godot --headless --path . res://tests/unit_tests.tscn
```

Full automated mission runs (no window; the "autopilot" replaces the keyboard):

```
# Solo: a strong rider should escape (exit code 0)...
godot --headless --path . -- --solo --autopilot=230 --timescale=4 --exit-on-results
# ...a weak rider should be caught (exit code 2)
godot --headless --path . -- --solo --autopilot=90 --timescale=4 --exit-on-results

# Two networked instances (run in two terminals):
godot --headless --path . -- --host --auto-start=2 --autopilot=220 --timescale=3 --exit-on-results
godot --headless --path . -- --join=127.0.0.1 --ftp=150 --autopilot=150 --timescale=3 --exit-on-results

# Endless Survival with a fixed world. The autopilot slows for survivors and
# crates, sprints past screamers and runners (with a limited sprint reserve)
# and drops back for exhausted survivors. Watch the log for "[Endless]" lines.
godot --headless --path . -- --solo --mode=endless --seed=7 --autopilot=150 --autopilot-pickup --autopilot-react=270 --timescale=10 --exit-on-results

# Criterium to 5 points; the autopilot races like a computer "sprinter".
# --log-race prints the bunch every 3 s (gaps, efforts, batteries).
godot --headless --path . -- --solo --mode=race --seed=3 --race-points=5 --autopilot=150 --autopilot-race=sprinter --timescale=10 --exit-on-results
```

`tests/run_all_tests.sh` runs all of the above (about 4 minutes; add
`--quick` for unit tests plus one short run per mode, under a minute). It runs
tests in parallel, and single-player runs use Godot's `--fixed-fps 60`: every
frame is exactly 1/60 s of game time and the game runs as fast as the computer
allows, e.g. `godot --headless --fixed-fps 60 --path . -- --solo --mode=race
...`. Only the multiplayer tests run in real time. Other options are listed at the
top of `scripts/autoload/dev_tools.gd`. For example, `-- --solo` skips the menu
when launching from a terminal.

## 6. Known limitations

- The trainer bridge has been confirmed working with a **Zwift Hub One** on
  Windows. Other FTMS / Cycling Power trainers should work but are untested.
- ANT+ (USB stick) isn't supported, only Bluetooth.
- Internet play is untested. LAN and same-machine play were tested.
- Players can't join a mission already in progress; join in the lobby.
- Placeholder visuals throughout. Sounds are real recordings from Freesound
  (see `audio/CREDITS.md`; one requires attribution).
- Zombies along the roadside are scenery. Only the horde can catch you.
- The criterium is single-player only (you against computer riders). Riders
  pass through each other (no collisions) and can't steer: the race is decided
  by effort, timing and slipstream.
